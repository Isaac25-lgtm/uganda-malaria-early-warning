Weekly Confirmed Malaria Cases and Forecast

Shows observed malaria cases and the forecast for the selected target week. Compare the target-week forecast with the district threshold to understand the risk assessment.

The selected count model predicts the target week only. Intermediate weekly forecasts are not generated, so no continuous forecast line is drawn. The shaded band marks the period between the issue week and the target week; it does not imply weekly predictions. Prediction intervals appear only when the selected count model publishes them.

Districts at Surge Risk

Ranks districts by forecast risk for the selected horizon. Predicted counts and surge risk may come from different validated models. They are displayed together for interpretation but are not combined into one model.

Risk level comes from the selected surge model, comparing its predicted cases with a district threshold built only from that district's history up to the issue week. The surge score is a difference in cases (predicted cases minus threshold), not a probability. Sort any column; search by district name.

Climate Predictors and Lags

Shows rainfall, temperature and humidity values used as supporting predictors in the forecasting models. Lag values show conditions from earlier weeks that were available before the forecast target.

Climate variables are supporting predictors associated with malaria patterns; they do not establish causation. National values are unweighted district means, so each district contributes equally. No lag is emphasised over another: the pipeline does not compute a climate–malaria correlation, so no "best" lag is claimed.

Model Performance Comparison

National rolling-origin validation metrics for the four approved models at the selected horizon. Count and surge models are selected on different rules and shown separately.

Metrics are national validation results across all districts; they do not change with the district filter. Count models are selected on lowest WAPE (RMSE when WAPE is unavailable). Surge models are selected from methods meeting the sensitivity and specificity guardrails where possible, then by highest F2 with sensitivity and PR-AUC as leading tie-breakers. Accuracy alone is never used, because surge weeks are rare and a model can look accurate while missing most surges.

Research use and interpretation

  • These outputs support review and early action. They are a research proof-of-concept, not an operational alert system.
  • A High or Very High risk level does not confirm an outbreak. It means predicted cases approach or exceed a district threshold derived from that district’s own recent history, and warrants review.
  • Predictions do not replace epidemiological judgement, field investigation or clinical confirmation.
  • DHIS2 reporting quality affects forecasts. Weeks with low reporting completeness may understate cases. Missing data is left missing and is never treated as zero.
  • Confirmed reporting errors are excluded from analytical outcomes only after documented district-level consultation; raw source values remain auditable.
  • Climate variables are associated predictors, not established causes. Association does not establish causation, and the pipeline does not compute a climate–malaria correlation.
  • Model quality is judged on error and surge-detection metrics together. Accuracy alone is not used, because surge weeks are rare and a model can look accurate while missing most surges.
